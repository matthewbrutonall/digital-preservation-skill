# Digital Preservation Glossary

Concise definitions for common terms used across this skill.

---

## Terms

**Access copy**  
A derivative version created for user access. It is usually easier to open or stream than the preservation master and may be compressed or converted to a more common format.

**Accession**  
The act of formally taking custody of material into an archive or records service, usually with an identifier and basic documentation.

**BagIt**  
A packaging specification that groups files with manifests and metadata so transfers can be verified and managed more reliably.

**Crawl**  
An automated web capture process that follows links and downloads pages and their associated resources within a defined scope.

**Bitstream**  
The sequence of bits that makes up a digital file. Bitstream preservation focuses on keeping those bits unchanged and recoverable.

**CDX index**  
An index of captured web resources used to support replay, lookup, and deduplication in web archives.

**Characterisation**  
The process of identifying and describing digital files, including their format, version, size, and other technical properties.

**Checksum**  
A value calculated from a file that acts like a fingerprint. If the file changes, the checksum changes.

**Disposal**  
The action taken at the end of a retention period. It may mean destruction, transfer to an archive, or review rather than automatic deletion.

**Digital Asset Register**  
A management record for collections or groups of digital material. It tracks ownership, size, formats, risks, rights, storage, and required actions.

**Disk imaging**  
Creating a sector-by-sector copy of a disk or other storage carrier. Useful when original structure or deleted data matters.

**DPC RAM**  
The Digital Preservation Coalition Rapid Assessment Model. A maturity model with eleven capability areas used to benchmark progress and plan improvements.

**DROID**  
A free file format identification tool from The National Archives. It uses PRONOM signatures to identify formats in batches.

**Fixity**  
Evidence that a file has remained unchanged. In practice this usually means verifying stored checksums over time.

**Legal hold**  
A direction to suspend normal disposal because records may be needed for litigation, investigation, audit, or a formal information request.

**Lossless / lossy**  
Lossless formats keep all information when saved or copied. Lossy formats discard some data, usually to reduce file size.

**Migration**  
Moving content from one format or environment to another to reduce obsolescence risk while trying to retain meaning and usability.

**METS**  
Metadata Encoding and Transmission Standard. An XML wrapper often used to package structural metadata and link to other metadata such as PREMIS.

**NDSA Levels**  
A staged framework for improving digital preservation practice, especially around storage, fixity, security, metadata, and file formats.

**OAIS**  
Open Archival Information System. A conceptual reference model describing the functions and information needed to preserve and provide access to digital material.

**PRONOM**  
A technical registry of file formats, software, and signatures used by tools such as DROID and Siegfried.

**PREMIS**  
The main preservation metadata standard. It focuses on objects, events, agents, and rights needed to document preservation activity.

**Preferred format**  
A format an organisation has chosen as lower risk and more sustainable for preservation or access. Preferred does not mean mandatory in every case.

**PII (Personally Identifiable Information)**  
Information that can be used to identify a specific individual. Detecting and managing PII is a critical part of appraisal and access workflows.

**Green Digital Preservation**  
The practice of reducing the environmental impact of digital preservation through rigorous appraisal, efficient storage, and sustainable hardware choices.

**Replay**  
Rendering a captured web archive so it can be viewed and used in a way that approximates the original live site.

**Records continuum model**  
A records management model that treats creation, capture, organisation, and pluralisation as ongoing and overlapping rather than a simple linear lifecycle.

**Retention schedule**  
A formal document that states how long categories of records are kept, what triggers that period, and what action happens at the end.

**Scope**  
The boundaries and rules that define what a web crawl should and should not capture.

**Seed**  
A starting URL supplied to a web crawler.

**Technology watch**  
Monitoring tools, formats, standards, suppliers, and community practice so new preservation risks can be spotted early.

**Vital records**  
Records essential to the continued operation or legal standing of an organisation during and after an emergency.

**WARC**  
Web ARChive format, the standard file format used to store captured web content and related metadata.

**Write blocker**  
Hardware or software that prevents changes being written back to source media during transfer or forensic work.

**Verifiable file manifest**  
A file-level list of filenames, paths, and checksums used to prove what was transferred and later verify integrity.

**Depositor**  
The person or organisation transferring digital material to an archive, records service, or preservation programme.

**Appraisal**  
The process of deciding what records or digital material should be kept, for how long, and for what value.

**Sensitive data**  
Information that requires controlled access because of privacy, confidentiality, safeguarding, security, or legal obligations.

**Emulation**  
Recreating an older hardware or software environment so legacy files or systems can still be accessed without changing the original files.
